On-device face comparison · Open source

A closer look that never leaves your device.

OpenWorld checks the faces in a photo or video you already have against public FBI missing-person and wanted-person posters. The comparison runs on your phone or computer. No account, no upload, no server.

What happens to a file

Six steps, all of them on your device.

The same library runs on iPhone, Android, Mac, Windows, and Linux. Each step can say no, and the result tells you which one did.

  1. Decode

    Every frame of the file is read into memory, one at a time. Frames are never written to disk.

    photo · video · GIF · HEIC

  2. Detect

    The YuNet detector looks for faces. A face is compared only when it is at least 112 pixels across in your file.

    YuNet 2023mar · MIT

  3. Gate

    A face that is turned away, blurred, too dark, or too bright is not compared. The result lists it and says why.

    pose · blur · exposure · landmarks

  4. Track

    A face in a video needs three good frames, or one second on screen, before it can become a candidate.

    3 frames or 1 s

  5. Compare

    The SFace network turns the face into numbers and compares them with every enabled poster, against a locked cutoff.

    SFace 2021dec · cutoff 0.63

  6. Result

    Possible candidate, no candidate, or incomplete. Never a raw score, and never a list of near misses.

    kept 7 days, then deleted

Privacy

Built to know as little as possible.

  • Nothing is uploaded

    Your file, the faces in it, and the result stay on the device. CI traces every network call a scan makes and fails if one reaches the internet.

  • No account, no analytics

    There is no sign-in, no tracking, no ads, and no crash reporter. OneX receives nothing.

  • No camera

    OpenWorld only reads a file you choose. It never records, and it does not run in the background.

  • No reports to anyone

    OpenWorld never contacts an agency. Opening a poster's page is a choice you make, in your browser.

  • Templates live in memory

    The numbers that describe a face are never saved. Results delete themselves after 7 days.

  • Open to inspection

    The code, the models, and every measurement are public. Builds are pinned and checked byte for byte.

Measured, with the limits

We publish what it gets wrong.

Measured on LFW, CALFW, and CPLFW: press photos of public figures, not FBI posters. Your results can be worse.

8 in 10MStrangers who pass the cutoff65 of 81,092,114 different-person pairs (LFW)
26%Same person, missed424 of 1,623 people, one photo pair each (LFW)
73%Missed when the photos are years apart2,190 of 3,000 same-person pairs (CALFW)

The cutoff is set where about one comparison in a million is a false match, because a long video compared with many posters makes millions of comparisons. Every result says how many false candidates chance alone would give that scan. Real FBI posters stay off until the same test on poster photos shows at most 2 false matches in a million, overall and for every group the posters describe.

If a result says “possible candidate”

It is not an identification.

  • Look-alikes, relatives, and old photos can pass the cutoff.
  • Do not approach, follow, confront, or try to hold anyone.
  • Do not post or share the result.
  • OpenWorld does not report anything. If someone is in immediate danger, call 911.
